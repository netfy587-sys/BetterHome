  <!-- DEFINE: HEALTHY FOOD -->
  <div class="define leaf">
    <div class="photo">
      <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1512621776951-a57141f2eefd?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop" alt="A bowl of fresh vegetable salad with tomatoes, chickpeas and greens">
      <div class="cap">Photo: Anna Pelzer / Unsplash</div>
    </div>
    <div class="text">
      <span class="tag">Side one</span>
      <h3>What counts as "healthy food"</h3>
      <p>Meals built mostly from whole, minimally processed ingredients — vegetables, fruit, whole grains, pulses, lean proteins, and modest amounts of fat, salt and sugar. It's usually cooked at home or in a kitchen you can see into, portioned with some intention, and eaten at a fairly predictable time.</p>
      <p>It isn't one cuisine or one diet plan. A plate of khichdi, a Mediterranean salad and a bowl of oats with fruit all qualify. What they share is a bias toward ingredients the body can use efficiently, and away from deep-frying, refined flour and heavy preservation.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- DEFINE: STREET FOOD -->
  <div class="define chili reverse">
    <div class="photo">
      <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1596522869169-95231d2b6864?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop" alt="Pooris being deep fried in oil at a roadside food stall in Uttar Pradesh, India">
      <div class="cap">Photo: Ashwini Chaudhary / Unsplash</div>
    </div>
    <div class="text">
      <span class="tag">Side two</span>
      <h3>What counts as "street food"</h3>
      <p>Food cooked and sold from stalls, carts and small roadside kitchens — pani puri, vada pav, momos, tacos, skewers, fried snacks — usually made fast, in full public view, and eaten standing up or on the move.</p>
      <p>It's defined less by its ingredients than by its setting: an open kitchen, a short wait, cash in hand, and a menu that rarely changes because the vendor has perfected three or four dishes over years, sometimes decades.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- BENEFITS -->
  <section class="benefits">
    <div class="wrap-wide">
      <p class="kicker">Give credit where it's due</p>
      <h2 class="section-title">What each one genuinely gets right</h2>

      <div class="benefit-cols">
        <div class="benefit-col leafcol">
          <h3>The case for healthy food</h3>

          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Steadier energy through the day</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Fibre and protein slow digestion, so blood sugar rises and falls more gradually than after a fried, sugar-heavy snack.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Lower long-term disease risk</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Diets built around vegetables, whole grains and lean protein are consistently linked to lower rates of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and obesity.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">You control the ingredients</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Salt, oil, sugar and portion size are your decisions, not a vendor's — useful for allergies, medical diets or simple preference.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Better for the gut long-term</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Fibre from vegetables, fruit and whole grains feeds gut bacteria in ways processed, fried food generally doesn't.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Cheaper over a month, not a meal</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Buying whole ingredients in bulk and cooking at home is usually less expensive across a week than eating out daily — even if any single street meal is cheap.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="benefit-col chilicol">
          <h3>The case for street food</h3>

          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Unmatched convenience</div>
            <div class="b-desc">No shopping, no cooking, no dishes — a full hot meal in the time it takes to cross the street.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Supports local livelihoods</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Street vendors are often small, independent operators; a plate bought from a stall supports one household directly.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Deep culinary skill, sharpened by repetition</div>
            <div class="b-desc">A vendor who has made the same dish for twenty years has usually refined it further than most home cooks ever will.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Cultural continuity</div>
            <div class="b-desc">Many street dishes carry regional history and technique that doesn't survive well in a home kitchen or restaurant menu.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="benefit-item">
            <div class="b-title">Genuinely low cost per plate</div>
            <div class="b-desc">For a single meal, right now, it's usually the cheapest hot food option available — important where budgets are tight.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <!-- RISKS -->
  <section class="risks">
    <div class="wrap-wide">
      <p class="kicker">The honest downside</p>
      <h2 class="section-title">What street food tends to cost you</h2>
      <p class="lede" style="margin-top:14px;">None of this means avoid it entirely — it means know what you're weighing.</p>

      <div class="risk-grid">
        <div class="risk-card">
          <h4>Reused, high-heat oil</h4>
          <p>Oil heated and reused repeatedly through a shift can break down into compounds linked to inflammation, and it's rarely visible from the customer side of the cart.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="risk-card">
          <h4>Hidden salt and sugar</h4>
          <p>Chutneys, sauces and batters often carry far more salt and sugar than the dish appears to, since both are cheap ways to boost flavour fast.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="risk-card">
          <h4>Inconsistent food safety</h4>
          <p>Water quality, ingredient storage and hand hygiene vary vendor to vendor — the same dish can be perfectly safe at one stall and risky at another.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="risk-card">
          <h4>Few vegetables, little protein</h4>
          <p>Most popular street dishes are built on refined carbohydrate and fried starch, with vegetables and protein playing a minor, garnish-level role.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="risk-card">
          <h4>Portion sizes you don't control</h4>
          <p>There's no nutrition label and no measuring cup — a "small" plate can carry more oil and calories than it looks like it should.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="risk-card">
          <h4>Easy to eat too often</h4>
          <p>Because it's fast, cheap and satisfying, street food is easy to default to daily — turning an occasional treat into most of a diet without noticing.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="tips">
    <div class="wrap">
      <p class="kicker">A more useful question than "which is better"</p>
      <h2 class="section-title">How to actually balance the two</h2>
      <p class="lede" style="margin-top:14px;">Most people don't need to quit street food. They need a few habits that make room for both.</p>

      <div class="tip-list">
        <div class="tip-row">
          <div class="tip-index">01</div>
          <div>
            <h4>Pick stalls by turnover, not appearance</h4>
            <p>A cart with a constant queue is refilling and reheating ingredients often — that matters more for safety than how clean the cart looks.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="tip-row">
          <div class="tip-index">02</div>
          <div>
            <h4>Add a vegetable side, on purpose</h4>
            <p>Most street meals are missing greens entirely. A side salad or a piece of fruit afterward closes that gap without changing what you ordered.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="tip-row">
          <div class="tip-index">03</div>
          <div>
            <h4>Treat fried items as the exception, not the default</h4>
            <p>Grilled, steamed or boiled street food — corn, momos, tikka — carries most of the flavour with a fraction of the oil.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="tip-row">
          <div class="tip-index">04</div>
          <div>
            <h4>Cook the predictable meals, eat out for the special ones</h4>
            <p>Let home cooking cover routine weekday meals, and save street food for when it's actually about the experience — not just convenience.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="tip-row">
          <div class="tip-index">05</div>
          <div>
            <h4>Ask what's in the chutney</h4>
            <p>The sauce is often where most of the salt, sugar and oil hides. A smaller portion of it changes the whole plate's balance.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <!-- VERDICT -->
  <section class="verdict">
    <div class="wrap">
      <p class="kicker">Where this leaves us</p>
      <h2 class="section-title">Not a winner. A ratio.</h2>
      <p class="verdict-quote">Healthy food builds the baseline your body runs on. Street food is one of the ways people actually enjoy eating — and cutting it out entirely rarely lasts. The real skill isn't choosing a side. It's noticing which one is quietly making up most of your week, and nudging that ratio back toward whole food without giving up the plate of chaat that makes Tuesday better.</p>
    </div>
